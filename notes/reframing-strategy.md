# Reframing Strategy: DIS → ISWC/IMWUT
*"Just a Glimpse: Designing Seamless Transitional Interfaces for Transient Mixed Reality Use"*

---

## Target Venue: ISWC 2026 Notes & Briefs (Primary)
**Deadline: May 17, 2026**

---

## What to Keep from the DIS Version

### ✅ Keep As-Is:
1. **The 6 prototypes** — the core empirical material; show-don't-tell
2. **The core insight:** transient scenarios require fundamentally different MR interface design than sustained-use HMD scenarios
3. **The designer interviews** — reframe as "expert evaluation" rather than design reflections
4. **The empirical specificity:** museums, exhibitions, classrooms as deployment contexts
5. **Key design principles** emerging from the annotated portfolio analysis

### ✅ Keep with Light Reframing:
1. **Annotated portfolios methodology** → reframe as "design research through iterative prototyping" (less theory-heavy language)
2. **RtD framing** → foreground it as ISWC's "Design Process for Wearables" category
3. **The six form factor categories** → organize explicitly as a **design space**

---

## What to Change

### ❌ Remove/Minimize:
- Long epistemological justification of annotated portfolios (DIS reviewers know it; ISWC reviewers don't care)
- Philosophical framing around design theory
- Extended related work sections that read as cultural studies
- CHI/DIS-specific reference chains

### ✏️ Reframe:
| DIS framing | ISWC/UbiComp framing |
|-------------|---------------------|
| "Annotated portfolio as epistemic contribution" | "Comparative prototype analysis revealing design principles" |
| "Design knowledge through RtD" | "Novel wearable form factor design for transient AR contexts" |
| "Seamful / seamless design" | "Transition latency and cognitive overhead in wearable MR onset" |
| "Situated design inquiry" | "In-context design evaluation at exhibition sites" |
| "Transient MR as design phenomenon" | "Transient MR as a distinct interaction class with specific design requirements" |

## What to Add for ISWC

### Minimal additions (sufficient for Notes/Briefs):
1. **Design space diagram (figure):** Map the 6 prototypes along 2–3 dimensions (e.g., portability vs. social visibility vs. interaction complexity). This shows systematic design thinking.
2. **Expert walkthrough summary:** 2–3 paragraph summary of designer interviews reframed as expert design evaluation (N=?, findings summary in bullet points)
3. **Comparison table:** Handheld vs. neck-worn vs. stationary — evaluate against Gemperle et al.'s wearability criteria and Profita et al.'s social acceptability findings
4. **Body Activity Zone Figure:** Apply Gemperle et al. (1998) [DOI: 10.1109/ISWC.1998.729537]'s body regions framework to justify form factor placement

### Optional (stronger paper, still within ISWC Notes scope):
- One brief deployment scenario: bring 2 prototypes to a local exhibition, collect qualitative observations (N=5–10 visitors, opportunistic)

---

## What to Add for IMWUT (if going that route)

### Required additions:
1. **Field deployment study:** Deploy 2–3 prototypes in real museum/exhibition context
   - Duration: 2–4 weeks
   - Participants: N=15–25 visitors (opportunistic sampling)
   - Data: interaction logging (counts, duration), exit interviews
2. **Controlled comparison study:** Have participants use all 3 form factor types on a standardized task
   - N=12–16
   - Measures: task completion time, glance count, subjective workload (NASA-TLX), social comfort (custom scale), preference
3. **Quantitative design space analysis:** Operationalize "transient" (interaction duration < X seconds), "seamless" (onset latency), "social acceptability" (self-report scale)
4. **Expanded paper length:** 25–35 pages; add Implementation section with technical specs

## How to Reframe Contributions

### DIS contribution framing:
> "We contribute design knowledge about transitional MR interfaces through an annotated portfolio of 6 prototypes, revealing how non-HMD form factors can support transient MR scenarios."

### ISWC contribution framing:
> "We present a design space for transient wearable MR, comprising three novel form factor categories (handheld, neck-worn, stationary) instantiated in six prototypes. Through expert design evaluation, we derive four design principles for transitional MR interfaces in public engagement contexts."

### IMWUT contribution framing:
> "We characterize transient MR interaction as a distinct class of wearable computing scenario and present empirical findings from a field deployment of three non-HMD MR prototypes in exhibition contexts (N=20 visitors). Our findings establish design guidelines for transient MR interfaces and show that non-HMD form factors outperform HMDs on social acceptability metrics in brief-encounter contexts."

## Structural Template for ISWC Notes (4–6 pages)

1. **Introduction** (0.5 page): Problem statement, transient MR scenarios, why non-HMD matters
2. **Related Work** (0.75 page): Transitional interfaces, wearable form factors, transient computing
3. **Design Space** (1 page): Three categories (handheld/neck-worn/stationary) + dimensions
4. **Prototypes** (1.5 pages): 6 prototypes briefly described with photos; 1–2 paragraphs each
5. **Expert Evaluation** (0.75 page): Method, participants (N=?), key themes
6. **Design Principles** (0.75 page): 4–5 principles for transient wearable MR
7. **Discussion & Conclusion** (0.5 page): Implications, limitations, future work
