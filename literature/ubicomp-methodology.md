# UbiComp/ISWC Methodology Expectations — Literature Review

What methodologies UbiComp/IMWUT and ISWC accept, with examples.

---

## Accepted Methodology Categories

### 1. System Papers with User Studies
The most common format in IMWUT. Implement a working system, conduct controlled lab study or deployment, report both system performance and user experience.

**Example:** Khurana, R., Goel, M., & Lyons, K. (2019). "Detachable Smartwatch." *IMWUT 3(3).* [DOI: 10.1145/3328921] — Prototype + user interviews + application demonstrations.

### 2. In-the-Wild Deployments
Highly valued in UbiComp. Deploy a system with real users in real contexts over extended time.

**Example:** Visuri, A., Sarsenbayeva, Z., van Berkel, N., Goncalves, J., Rawassizadeh, R., Kostakos, V., & Ferreira, D. (2017). "Quantifying Sources and Types of Smartwatch Usage Sessions." *CHI 2017.* [DOI: 10.1145/3025453.3025817] — Longitudinal smartwatch usage logging.

### 3. Large-Scale Behavioral Studies
Using sensing, logging, or surveys at scale (N > 100).

**Example:** Sahami Shirazi, A. et al. (2014). "Large-Scale Assessment of Mobile Notifications." *CHI 2014.* [DOI: 10.1145/2556288.2557189] — 30K+ notification events.

### 4. Design Explorations with Evaluation
RtD or design-led work paired with empirical grounding — especially in IMWUT where pure RtD is less common than at DIS.

**Example:** Dunne, L.E., & Smyth, B. (2007). "Psychophysical Elements of Wearability." *CHI 2007.* [DOI: 10.1145/1240624.1240674] — Design framework + participant evaluation.

### 5. ISWC Design Track (most relevant for this paper)
ISWC explicitly accepts:
- **Research through Design** — prototyping + reflection
- **Craft and Computing** — material exploration + design insights
- **Culturally-Sensitive Design** — contextual design processes
- **Design and Conceptual Innovation** — novel form factor proposals with design rationale

This track does NOT require quantitative user studies. Expert evaluation, designer reflection, and annotated portfolio methods are acceptable.

**Key ISWC papers using design methods:**
- Gemperle, F. et al. (1998). "Design for Wearability." *ISWC 1998.* [DOI: 10.1109/iswc.1998.729537] — Design principles paper, no formal user study.
- Dunne, L.E. (2010). "The Hidden Machine: Wearable Technology and the Body." *ISWC 2010.* [UNVERIFIED] — Design critique/provocation.

## How to Frame This Paper for ISWC

### What to Emphasize:
1. **Design Process:** The annotated portfolio methodology as a rigorous design research method — not just "here are some prototypes"
2. **Form Factor Innovation:** Each prototype as a distinct wearable design contribution with specific form factor rationale
3. **Designer Interviews as Expert Evaluation:** Reframe interviews as expert design evaluation — this is an accepted method at ISWC
4. **Design Principles:** Extract 4–6 clear, actionable design principles for transient wearable MR
5. **Design Space:** Frame the 6 prototypes as systematically exploring a design space (dimensions: portability × visibility × social context)

### What to De-emphasize:
- DIS-specific theoretical framing (annotated portfolios as epistemology)
- Cultural/aesthetic critique language
- Long philosophical sections

### What to Add (minimal, for ISWC Notes 4–6 pages):
- Optional: brief expert walkthrough (N=5–8 HCI designers reviewing prototypes)
- Optional: one in-context scenario observation (museum/exhibition visit with prototype)
- Required: clear design space visualization (figure showing the 3 form factor types × use contexts)

## IMWUT Requirements (if targeting full paper)

### Minimum Empirical Requirements:
- N ≥ 12 participants in controlled study OR
- N ≥ 30 in field deployment OR
- Mixed: formative study (N=6–8) + summative evaluation (N=12+)

### Recommended Additions for IMWUT:
1. Deploy 2–3 most promising prototypes in a real exhibition/museum setting
2. Log interaction data: glance duration, number of interactions, abandonment rate
3. Exit interviews: N=15–20 visitors, 15 min semi-structured
4. Measure: task completion, social comfort, preference ranking across form factors

### Estimated Timeline for IMWUT (Aug cycle):
- March–May: Build/refine 2 key prototypes for deployment
- June: Field deployment (museum/gallery, 2–3 week run)
- July: Analysis + writing
- Aug 1: Submit
