# Exhibition and Museum Wearable Deployments

*Real-world instances of temporal wearing in public settings*

## 1. Overview

Exhibition and museum contexts represent organic, pre-theoretical instances of temporal wearing. Visitors routinely don devices briefly, use them at specific exhibits, and return or remove them. These practices exist without the theoretical framework that "temporal wearing" provides.

## 2. Museum AR Deployments

### 2.1 Visitors' Acceptance of Wearable AR Technology in Museums (Li et al., 2024) [DOI: 10.1177/21582440241303507]
**Venue:** SAGE Open, 14(4)

Large-scale study of visitor acceptance of wearable AR in museum settings. Found that perceived usefulness and ease of use drive acceptance, but social influence and facilitating conditions also play significant roles.

**Temporal wearing lens:** Museum AR devices are inherently temporal — visitors don't wear AR devices throughout their museum visit. They use them at specific exhibits, creating natural temporal wearing patterns.

### 2.2 vom Lehn, Heath, & Hindmarsh (2001) [DOI: 10.1525/si.2001.24.2.189] — Exhibiting Interaction
**Venue:** Symbolic Interaction, 24(2)

Ethnographic study of conduct and collaboration in museums and galleries. Key findings:
- Museum interactions are **brief and episodic** — visitors move between exhibits with short dwell times
- Interactions are **socially organized** — visitors negotiate access to exhibits, point things out to companions, and create shared viewing moments
- **The body is central** — visitors use bodily orientation, gesture, and positioning to organize their encounter with exhibits

**Temporal wearing connection:** Museum interaction is already temporally structured in seconds-to-minutes episodes. Temporal wearing devices align naturally with this rhythm.

### 2.3 Mistry & Maes (2009) [DOI: 10.1145/1667146.1667160] — SixthSense
**Venue:** SIGGRAPH '09 Emerging Technologies

Neck-worn wearable gestural interface projecting information onto surfaces. While designed for continuous wear, the actual use pattern in demonstrations was temporal: users activated it for specific tasks.

**Temporal wearing lens:** SixthSense's neck-worn form factor enables rapid transitions between active and passive states, embodying temporal wearing principles even though it was designed for permanent wear.

## 3. Exhibition VR Experiences

### 3.1 Pop-up VR Installations
Commercial VR exhibitions (e.g., teamLab, The Infinite, various art fairs) typically feature:
- **Queue → don → experience (1-5 min) → doff → move on** — a compressed session wear approaching temporal wear
- Attendants assist with donning/doffing, minimizing transition friction
- Hygiene protocols (face wipes, disposable covers) indicate awareness of the temporal nature

### 3.2 Art Exhibition AR Interventions
AR art exhibitions (e.g., KAWS AR installations, Acute Art collaborations) often use personal smartphones as temporal wearing devices — visitors hold up phones briefly to view augmented content, then lower them.

**Insight:** Handheld AR in exhibitions is already temporal wearing, just not theorized as such.

## 4. Classroom and Educational Deployments

### 4.1 Brief AR Encounters in Education
Educational AR deployments (e.g., Merge Cube, AR anatomy viewers) often follow temporal wearing patterns:
- Students pick up a device, view AR content for seconds, put it down
- Pass-around protocols create natural temporal wearing sequences
- Brief encounters support focused learning moments without sustained device commitment

## 5. Public Display and Situated Installations

### 5.1 Interactive Public Displays with Wearable Components
Some public installations incorporate wearable elements designed for temporal use:
- **Viewfinders/stereoscopes** at tourist sites — temporal wearing predecessors (coin-operated, time-limited)
- **Interactive museum stations** — visitors step up, engage briefly, move on

### 5.2 Historical Precedents
- **Opera glasses/lorgnettes** — The historical model of temporal wearing: raised to eyes for seconds during a performance, lowered, raised again. No one wears opera glasses continuously.
- **Binoculars at overlooks** — Coin-operated viewing binoculars at scenic points are perhaps the purest temporal wearing artifact.
- **Kaleidoscopes and stereoscopes** — Handheld optical devices designed for brief, repeated encounters.

## 6. Key Observations

1. **Temporal wearing is already practiced** — it exists in museums, exhibitions, classrooms, and public spaces
2. **It lacks theoretical articulation** — no existing work names or formalizes these patterns
3. **Design is often ad hoc** — devices used temporally weren't necessarily designed for temporal wearing
4. **Our prototypes deliberately design for temporal wearing** — making our contribution the gap between practice and theory
