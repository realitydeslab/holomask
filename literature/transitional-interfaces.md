# Transitional Interfaces — Literature Review

Papers on transitional interfaces, fluid interactions, and seamless AR/MR transitions.

---

## Core Papers

### 1. Dhaka, P., Katsuragawa, K., & Hasan, K. (2024)
**"Exploring Augmented Reality User Interface Transitions Across Mid-Air, On-Body and Physical Surfaces"**
*IEEE ISMAR 2024*
DOI: 10.1109/ismar62088.2024.00134

**Key contribution:** Investigates seamless transitions of AR interfaces between mid-air, on-body (wrist), and physical surfaces. Defines a design space for transitional AR UI placement. Shows users prefer smooth transitions over modal switches.

**Relevance:** Closest technical analog to the paper. Directly addresses transitional AR UI. Cited by 4 within a year—suggests emerging area. Our work adds the transient use scenario and non-HMD form factor perspective.

---

### 2. Jannat, M.E., Shah, S.M., & Hasan, K. (2025)
**"Exploring Body-Anchored Augmented Reality Interfaces Across Different Mobility and Social Contexts"**
*IEEE ISMAR 2025* [DOI: 10.1109/ismar67309.2025.00147]

**Key contribution:** Studies where users prefer to anchor AR interfaces on the body across different contexts (sitting, walking, social settings). Finds context-sensitive preferences for body placement.

**Relevance:** Validates our hypothesis that body placement matters in transient MR. Provides empirical benchmark for understanding social acceptability of AR on-body anchoring.

---

### 3. Khurana, R., Goel, M., & Lyons, K. (2019)
**"Detachable Smartwatch"**
*Proc. ACM IMWUT 3(3)*
DOI: 10.1145/3328921

**Key contribution:** Proposes a watch that morphs between wrist-worn and handheld modes, retaining glanceability for micro-interactions while enabling complex extended interactions when detached. User interviews reveal distinct contexts and preferences.

**Relevance:** Directly parallels our "transient" framing — the smartwatch's "glanceable" mode is equivalent to our transient MR interaction mode. Provides vocabulary: micro-interactions vs. extended engagement. Published in IMWUT, demonstrating this type of contribution has precedent.

---

### 4. Benko, H., Wilson, A.D., & Zannier, F. (2014)
**"Dyadic Projected Spatial Augmented Reality"**
*ACM UIST 2014* [DOI: 10.1145/2642918.2647402]

**Key contribution:** Projected AR without HMDs for co-located collaboration. System projects onto physical surfaces and objects, enabling seamless physical-digital integration.

**Relevance:** Establishes projection-based MR as a non-HMD alternative. Relevant to our stationary MR prototype.

---

### 5. Rekimoto, J. (1997)
**"NaviCam: A Magnification Lens for Video-Based Augmented Reality"**
*Presence: Teleoperators and Virtual Environments 6(4)* [DOI: 10.1162/pres.1997.6.4.399]

**Key contribution:** Handheld magnifying lens metaphor for mobile AR, using a camera-based interface to reveal digital overlays on physical objects. One of the earliest handheld AR demonstrations.

**Relevance:** Historical grounding for the handheld AR form factor. Our paper can position itself as revisiting/refining this handheld metaphor for transient contexts.

---

### 6. Weiser, M. (1991)
**"The Computer for the 21st Century"**
*Scientific American 265(3)* [DOI: 10.1038/scientificamerican0991-94]

**Key contribution:** Foundational vision of ubiquitous computing — calm technology that recedes into the background and comes forward only when needed.

**Relevance:** The philosophical anchor for "transient" computing. MR that appears briefly and seamlessly maps to Weiser's vision of technology that fits the periphery.

---

## Notes for Paper Positioning

- The "transitional interface" concept is emerging (Dhaka 2024 is the clearest precursor)
- Our contribution is to extend from "transition between surfaces" to "transition between real-world and MR" in transient social contexts
- The key gap: prior work focuses on efficiency of UI transitions; we focus on the *experiential* and *social* quality of MR onset/offset
